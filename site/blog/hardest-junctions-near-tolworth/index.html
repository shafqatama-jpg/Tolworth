The 7 Hardest Junctions Near Tolworth Test Centre

Some junctions around Tolworth catch out even confident learners. Here are seven that our pupils find hardest, and how to handle each one calmly.

Some junctions around Tolworth catch out even confident learners. Here are seven that our pupils find hardest, and how to handle each one calmly.

1. The Tolworth roundabout on the A3

The big junction where the A3 meets the A240 is busy, fast-moving and has several lanes. The secret is preparation: read the signs and road markings well before you arrive, choose your lane early and stay in it all the way round. Look well ahead for gaps rather than staring at the car next to you.

2. Joining the A3 from a slip road

Slip roads are there to help you match the speed of traffic on the main road. Many learners join too slowly, which is more dangerous than joining confidently. Check your mirror and blind spot early, build up speed along the slip road and merge into a safe gap — without stopping at the end unless you really have to.

Hesitating halfway across is worse than waiting another few seconds.

3. Leaving the A3

Getting off is just as important. Spot your exit sign early, check mirrors, signal in good time and move into the deceleration lane before you slow down. Then watch your speed: you'll often arrive straight into a lower limit and a roundabout.

4. Hook roundabout (the "Ace of Spades")

Where the A3 meets the A243 at Hook is a large, busy junction that many locals still call the Ace of Spades. With several exits close together, signalling at the right moment matters. Know which exit you want, keep checking your mirrors as lanes split and merge, and signal left after you pass the exit before yours.

5. Tolworth Broadway

It's not a single junction, but the Broadway packs a lot into a short stretch: bus stops, pedestrian crossings, side roads, delivery vehicles and parked cars. Keep your speed down, scan for pedestrians stepping out between vehicles, and give cyclists plenty of room.

6. Busy crossroads on the A240

Turning right across traffic on a busy main road tests your judgement of speed and distance. Position correctly, wait with your wheels straight, and only go when you're sure the gap is big enough — hesitating halfway across is worse than waiting another few seconds.

7. Narrow residential streets with parked cars

Quiet streets in Tolworth, Surbiton and Berrylands can be the trickiest of all. With cars parked on both sides, you need to plan who gives way. Look far ahead, find a safe place to wait early, and acknowledge drivers who let you through.

How to master them

Every one of these junctions gets easier with repetition. Our instructors build them into your lessons one at a time, then combine them in mock tests so test day feels routine. Read our full Tolworth Test Centre guide for an interactive map.

Frequently asked questions

Which junction do most learners fail on at Tolworth?

DVSA doesn't publish faults by junction. In our experience, big multi-lane roundabouts and joining fast roads cause the most nerves — which is why we practise them most.

Will I definitely drive on the A3 on my test?

Not necessarily. Routes vary, but the A3 and its junctions can feature, so it's important to be ready for them.

Can you take me round these junctions before my test?

Yes — a mock test or a test cover warm-up lesson can include the junctions you're worried about.
