Tolworth Driving Test Routes Explained (2026)

Tolworth tests mix busy town roads, fast dual carriageway and quiet residential streets. Here's what that means for you — and how to prepare so nothing on the day feels new.

Tolworth tests mix busy town roads, fast dual carriageway and quiet residential streets. Here's what that means for you — and how to prepare so nothing on the day feels new.

Do test routes still exist?

DVSA stopped publishing official test routes years ago, and examiners can vary the roads they use. So when people talk about "the Tolworth test routes", they really mean the mix of roads, junctions and hazards that commonly come up on tests that start from Tolworth Driving Test Centre.

That's good news. You don't need to memorise a route — you need to be confident on the types of road the area throws at you. If you can handle those calmly, the exact turns don't matter.

What the test looks like

The practical car test lasts about 40 minutes. It starts with an eyesight check (reading a number plate from 20 metres) and a "tell me" vehicle safety question. Then you'll drive on a mix of roads, including around 20 minutes of independent driving where you follow a sat nav or traffic signs.

You'll also be asked to do one manoeuvre — a parallel park, parking in a bay (driving in and reversing out, or reversing in and driving out), or pulling up on the right and reversing for about two car lengths. Some tests include an emergency stop. While you're driving, the examiner will ask one "show me" question, such as how you'd demist the windscreen.

To pass you need 15 or fewer driving faults and no serious or dangerous faults.

The kinds of roads around Tolworth

Fast roads. The A3 Kingston bypass runs right through the area. Joining it from a slip road, holding a steady speed, keeping a safe gap and leaving again in good time are skills that can come up on a Tolworth test.

Big roundabouts. Where the A3 meets local roads there are large, multi-lane roundabouts. Reading the lane markings early and staying in your lane is essential.

Busy town roads. Tolworth Broadway and the main roads towards Surbiton and Kingston bring buses, cyclists, pedestrian crossings and parked cars on both sides.

Residential streets. Narrow roads with parked cars test your judgement when meeting oncoming traffic — and they're where most manoeuvres happen.

How we prepare you on the local roads

Our instructors teach around Tolworth every day, so lessons naturally build towards these roads. We start somewhere quiet, then add one new challenge at a time: junctions, then roundabouts, then busier roads, then the dual carriageway.

As your test gets close we switch to test-style driving: longer stretches of independent driving with a sat nav, every manoeuvre, show me / tell me practice and at least one full mock test marked like the real thing.

Instructor tip: Ask for a lesson at the same time of day as your test. Traffic around Tolworth is very different at 8am and 2pm.

Five things to practise before test day

  1. Slip roads — use the whole slip road to match the traffic speed.
  2. Lane discipline on multi-lane roundabouts.
  3. Speed limit changes — they come thick and fast around Tolworth; keep checking the signs.
  4. Meeting traffic in narrow streets — plan early, be decisive, be courteous.
  5. Sat nav driving — if you take a wrong turn, it's not a fault on its own as long as you drive safely.

Frequently asked questions

Can I practise the exact Tolworth test route?

No one can promise the exact route — DVSA doesn't publish them and examiners vary them. We practise the roads and junctions that most often feature, so you're ready for any of them.

How long is the driving test?

About 40 minutes, including around 20 minutes of independent driving.

Do I need to take my test at Tolworth?

No — you can book any centre, but practising where you'll be tested makes a big difference. Since 9 June 2026 you can only move a booked test to one of the 3 nearest centres, so choose carefully.
